“I told you about the leak in March.”

Prove it. Paper Trail keeps a dated, shared record of a rented home — what condition it was in, what you reported, when you reported it, and what the landlord said back.

The problem

Almost every dispute between a renter and a landlord comes down to a question of what happened, and when. Was that stain there on move-in day? Did anyone mention the boiler before winter? Was the repair promised in writing?

Today that history lives in a text thread, a phone call nobody wrote down, and a folder of photos with no dates anyone trusts. When a deposit is on the line, neither side can prove much of anything — so whoever is more confident usually wins.

How it works

  1. Record the condition. Room by room, with photos, in the first week you move in. This is the five minutes that saves the argument two years later.
  2. Report through the log. Every issue you raise gets a date stamp and goes to the landlord as a link — no account, no app for them to install.
  3. Both sides see the same page. The landlord can acknowledge, schedule, or close an issue. Every action lands in the same record. Nothing can be edited after the fact — corrections are added, not swapped in.
  4. Leave with the whole story. At move-out, the log assembles into one printable pack: original condition, every report, every reply, next to whatever is being deducted.

Who is who

  • Tenant

    Keeps the log

    Records condition, reports issues, and owns the record at move-out.

  • Landlord or agent

    Replies by link

    Opens one link, sees only their tenancy, answers in the log. No signup.

  • Contractor

    Sees one job

    A scoped link to the single issue they were hired for — nothing else.

  • Deposit scheme or court

    Reads the pack

    Gets a read-only export. It cannot be edited to suit whoever sent it.

What this is not

Paper Trail is a record, not a lawyer and not a verdict. It does not tell you your rights, it does not decide who is owed what, and a neat log is not a guarantee that anyone rules in your favour.

It also only holds what you actually put in it. A log that starts on move-out day proves very little — which is exactly why the condition report comes first.
